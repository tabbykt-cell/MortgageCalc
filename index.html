Estimates only. Actual quotes depend on your lender, credit profile, escrow rules, and location. PMI and FHA MIP rates vary by lender, credit score, and LTV — the defaults shown are editable placeholders, not quoted rates, and you should verify current figures with a lender. Cancellation rules are modeled simply: conventional PMI ends when the balance reaches your chosen LTV of the original price; FHA MIP follows the duration you select. Property tax and insurance are assumed flat over the life of the loan, which real escrow payments are not — so the "total paid" figure understates the true escrow total in most markets. ARM projections are scenarios, not forecasts: nobody can predict the index. The worst case assumes every adjustment hits its cap; the expected case assumes your index-plus-margin estimate; the best case assumes no change at all. Real ARMs also vary in index, margin, adjustment frequency, floors, and rounding rules — read your own term sheet. FHA and conventional loan limits vary by county and change annually. This calculator DOES check them: conforming limits come from the official FHFA 2026 county file (nationwide), and FHA limits from HUD's CY2026 data where HUD publishes it — elsewhere the FHA answer is reported as unknown rather than guessed. The 28/36 and FHA 31/43 ratios are widely used rules of thumb, not underwriting decisions — lenders weigh credit, reserves, employment and program rules, and they measure gross income, so your after-tax picture is tighter than the percentages imply. Home value is projected by compounding the appreciation rate you enter. It is an assumption you control, not a forecast; real housing markets are local, volatile, and can fall. Equity shown here is before selling costs. Note also that PMI removal based on appreciation normally requires a lender-approved appraisal, which this model does not attempt. The "Ownership cost and projected equity" section is not an investment, ROI, or rent-vs-buy analysis — it excludes maintenance, repairs, selling costs, the opportunity cost of cash tied up in the down payment, rent avoided, and taxes.
